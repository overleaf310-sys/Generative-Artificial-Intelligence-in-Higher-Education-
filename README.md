The rapid adoption of Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) tools is reshaping educational practices in higher education, highlighting the need to critically evaluate their educational value, responsible use, and implications for student learning. This study investigated university students’ awareness, trust, ethical concerns, usage patterns, and willingness to use GenAI tools. A cross-sectional survey was conducted among 500 bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral students from Law, Computer Science, Chemistry, and Physics at a public university in Pakistan. Data were collected using a structured questionnaire and analyzed through descriptive statistics, reliability analysis, group comparisons, correlation analysis, multiple regression, and bootstrap mediation analysis. Overall, 78% of respondents had prior GenAI experience, with ChatGPT being the most widely used tool. Awareness, trust, ethical concerns, and willingness were moderate. Prior AI users showed significantly higher awareness, trust, and willingness, while willingness also varied by discipline and AI-use frequency (p < .001). The regression model explained 23% of willingness, with trust (β = .264) and awareness (β = .246) as the strongest positive predictors and ethical concerns as a negative predictor (β = −.128). Trust also significantly mediated the relationship between awareness and willingness (indirect effect = 0.056, 95% CI [0.030, 0.086]). These findings highlight the importance of strengthening AI literacy, informed trust, output verification, ethical guidance, and discipline-specific policies for the responsible integration of GenAI in higher education.
